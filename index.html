Why the Gladiator JT Needs Its Own Seat Covers

The Jeep Gladiator JT shares its platform with the Wrangler JL, but Jeep made meaningful changes to the seating architecture when they added the pickup bed. Here's what's different and why it matters for seat cover selection:

Front seats: The Gladiator JT's front seats have longer rails than the Wrangler JL to accommodate the extended cab footwell. Seat covers that use under-seat strap anchoring may not reach correctly if they're patterned on the JL. Custom-fit JT covers anchor at the correct points.

Rear bench: This is the biggest difference. The Wrangler JL has a fold-and-tumble rear seat that folds completely flat for cargo. The Gladiator JT has a conventional truck-style rear bench that folds forward but does not tumble. The bench cushion profile, mounting points, and seatback dimensions are different. A Wrangler JL rear cover will not fit properly on the JT bench.

Trim variation: The JT was offered in Sport, Sport S, Overland, Rubicon, Mojave, and Willys trims from 2020–2026. The seat base design is consistent across trims — but some premium trims have additional bolstering or seating sensors. Custom-fit covers from Bartact account for these variations.

JT production span: The Jeep Gladiator JT launched for the 2020 model year and continued through 2026 with minimal seat architecture changes. A quality custom-fit cover for 2020 will work correctly on a 2026 model.

How to Install JT Seat Covers — What to Expect

Installing seat covers on the Gladiator JT is a DIY-friendly job for most products in this guide. Here's what to expect:

Front seats (30–60 min each): Remove headrests first — they pull straight up. Slip the seat back cover over the top, work it down the edges, tuck and secure the bottom straps under the seat. The front seat base cover slips over the cushion and anchors under the rails. No tools required for most covers.

Rear bench (45–75 min): The Gladiator JT's rear bench is a two-piece design — a separate bottom cushion and a folding seatback. Remove both headrests, cover the seatback first, then fit the bottom cushion cover. The bench doesn't tumble like a Wrangler's, so it stays in place while you work.

Bartact covers (1–2 hours): Bartact's custom-fit covers require more time because of the precision fit. They ship with detailed installation instructions. The result is a factory-look finish that doesn't bunch or slip. Worth the extra time if you want it done right.

Pro tip: Pull the bottom cushion slightly forward before fitting the rear bench cover — it makes tucking the front edge of the bottom cover much easier on the JT's bench design.

Care & Maintenance for JT Seat Covers

How long your JT seat covers last depends largely on how you maintain them. Here's what works for each material type:

Cordura polyester (Bartact): Brush off trail dust, spot clean with mild soap and water. Machine washable on cold gentle cycle — air dry only, never machine dry. UV treatment can be refreshed annually with a 303 Aerospace Protectant application.

Neoprene: Rinse with fresh water after wet trail use to prevent salt or mineral deposits. Wipe clean with a damp cloth. Can be machine washed on gentle — air dry flat. Avoid petroleum-based cleaners, which degrade neoprene over time.

Faux leather: Wipe down regularly with a damp microfiber cloth. Use a leather/vinyl conditioner every 3–6 months to prevent cracking, especially in desert or high-UV climates. Avoid alcohol-based cleaners which dry out the PU surface.

General rule: Don't let mud sit for weeks — it stiffens fabric fibers and accelerates wear. A quick hose-down after trail runs will extend cover life by years.
